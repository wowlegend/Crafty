The light and colour script

6752ce5con main 17:35Z · new
  • Night is moonlit, not navy. A six-key colour script sets the light by the sun’s phase, and below the horizon the moon takes the key light on its own vector (01fb8a88, 452d19cc).
  • One sky frame. The light rig, the dome, the sun billboard, the grade and the land read one frame, so the sun billboard finally arcs (1ab2e4d0). The sky follows the game clock every frame, so dawn and dusk ramp instead of stepping once a second (43cc3112). Storm clouds darken again (212d294d).
  • Fireflies at night, nothing on the lens by day. The motes used to float on the camera by day. Now they are fireflies, full at night, 0.6 at dusk, 0.3 before dawn and none from sunrise to sunset, and each fades out between 6 and 2.5 m from the eye (037ce81a).
  • A day lasts 480 s, set by one constant (14c9707b, item 21). A fresh profile sees its first dusk at 4:00 of play and its first dawn at 8:00, so the First Night fits ten minutes. Golden hour’s blend lasts 16.8 s, up from 10.5, and the dusk warning comes 43 s before night, up from 27. Saves load unchanged. Exception: the spawner runs on real time, so a night now holds the siege at its mob cap for 240 s, not 150. Night 1 ships at that length unmeasured; W3-A rebalances it.
  • The ground reads at dawn, golden hour and through the twilight blends, and the pad by the Hearth no longer turns maroon at dusk (73f4cebc). The review found the first keys lit dawn and golden hour darker than midnight and than main; after the retune the model’s grey card reads L 0.418 at dawn, 0.415 at golden hour, 0.381 at the dusk blend and 0.337 at midnight, against main’s 0.374, 0.374, 0.146 and 0.146. Exception: at midnight the ground within about 1 m of the brazier still reads on the maroon line, as it did before this lane (a registry row).
  • Not in this landing: bloom only for HDR and the sun-glow fog (the light plan’s Tasks 5 and 6), which wait for W2-I to measure the passes they touch. Not looked at on the live site.

d201c073on main 16:22Z · new

Up close nothing changes: magnification stays NEAREST, and a face seen head-on keeps its crisp texels out to about 16 to 28 blocks.
